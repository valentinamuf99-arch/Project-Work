Chosen moment

Before boarding. From days ahead up to the platform, when the commuter needs to know in advance whether their train is late, cancelled or affected by a strike, and decide what to do.

The moment covers three points in time: days before the trip (#4), at home before leaving (#1, #7), and at the station before boarding (#2, #8). It involves four participants: P2, P3, P4, P6.

#1 · Early commuter, before leaving home at six

HMW let an early commuter know, before leaving home at six, that their first train is already late or cancelled, so they can choose whether to go to the station at all?

Wake-up check. A notification at a time the commuter picks (e.g. 5:40) that gives their train’s status in one line: on time, late by X, or cancelled.
Delay threshold alert. The commuter sets a limit (e.g. 20 minutes). They hear nothing unless the delay passes it, so silence means “go”.
Real-time train view. A live view of the train’s position on its way from the earlier stations (e.g. Bassano), with a delay estimate for the commuter’s stop.
Go / don’t go card. A home-screen widget that combines the train’s status with the next alternative (later train, bus) and suggests whether to leave now.
Alarm clock link. The phone alarm shifts later automatically if the first train is cancelled or very late.
Instant delay push. (yours) A push notification sent as soon as the commuter’s train is reported late or cancelled.
Delay and cancellation odds. (yours) A percentage showing how likely the train is to be late or cancelled today.
Reports from earlier stops. (yours) Commuters waiting at earlier stations report whether the train has passed and whether it is late.
#2 · Cancelled or only late

HMW let an office commuter know, as soon as it is decided, whether their train is cancelled or only late, when live tracking shows it neither departed nor cancelled?

Status in plain words. Replace “neither departed nor cancelled” with a clear state: “Waiting for a decision”, “Late, expected at…”, “Cancelled”.
Decision push. A notification sent the moment the operator decides, so the commuter does not have to keep refreshing.
Likelihood label. While the state is unclear, show how often this situation ends in a cancellation on this route (e.g. “usually cancelled after 20 min”).
Decision deadline. Tell the commuter by when the operator will decide (“update by 9:45”), so they know how long to wait before acting.
Staff-confirmed status. Station staff or the conductor can confirm the state in the system, which updates every channel at once.
#4 · Strike days ahead

HMW make sure an evening student learns from the service, days ahead, that a strike hits their route and which of their trains still run?

Strike notice for my trip. (merged with yours) An alert a few days before, sent to commuters whose saved route, ticket or pass is hit by the strike, with the strike’s hours.
Guaranteed trains list. For the commuter’s usual trains, a clear mark: guaranteed, at risk, or not running.
Calendar entry. The strike and the guaranteed trains are added to the commuter’s calendar automatically, with a reminder the day before.
Station and on-board posters. Notices on boards and on trains of the route, with a QR code for the list of guaranteed trains.
Plan for the day. A suggested itinerary for the strike day: which guaranteed train to take, or which bus to use.
Strikes section. (yours) A dedicated “Strikes” section in the service listing upcoming strikes and the routes they hit.
Strike email with train table. (yours) An email announcing the strike, with a table of the commuter’s affected and guaranteed trains.
#7 · When my train reaches my stop

HMW let a pass holder know, before going to the station, when their own train will reach their stop, when the operator’s app does not say whether it runs?

Train number lookup. Search by train number and see its real-time passage through each stop (like the ViaggiaTreno site, but readable).
Saved usual train. The pass holder saves their regular train once; the app opens on its live arrival time at their stop.
Leave-home reminder. A notification telling when to leave home, based on the train’s real arrival at the stop and the walk to the station.
Pass-linked trip. Linking the pass to its route, so the app knows the commuter’s stops without a ticket purchase.
Message lookup. An SMS or chat service: send the train number, get back the expected time at your stop.
#8 · Will the delay pass or grow

HMW help a student at the station, finding their usual train late, judge whether the delay will pass or grow, while there is still time to find another way?

Reason on the board. The station board shows the cause of the delay next to the minutes (e.g. “technical fault”, “previous train late”).
Trend arrow. Next to the delay, an arrow showing whether it has been growing, stable, or shrinking in the last minutes.
Announcement with outlook. Station announcements say not only “late by X” but whether more delay is expected.
Help point at the station. A screen or staff desk where you can ask about one train and get the alternatives (next train, bus).
“Alternative from here” on the board. When a train passes a delay threshold, the board shows the next way to the same destination.
