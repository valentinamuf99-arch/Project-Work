# Ideation · twelve concepts per question, before boarding

## Scope and method

This note widens the range of answers to five How-Might-We questions from `2026-10-26-01.1-how-might-we-ai.md`: #1 (F1 · P2), #2 (F3 · P2), #4 (F4 · P4), #7 (F8 · P3) and #8 (F9 · P6). It replaces the previous version of this file, which covered other questions; none of its concepts carry over by default.

- **The moment.** Before boarding: from days ahead up to the platform, when the commuter needs to know whether their train is late, cancelled or hit by a strike, and decide what to do. A concept that only works on board or after getting off is out of the moment and was dropped.
- **Twelve concepts per question.** The number before the dot names the question the concept answers (`1.4` answers #1).
- **A direction** is a family of answers, not a variation of one answer. The same direction recurs across questions; the eleven directions are listed in the Directions unit.
- **One line per concept**, saying what it does, not how.
- **`[also mine]`** marks a concept that gives the same answer as one in `2026-10-26-02-ideation-human.md`, whatever the wording, and whichever question it sits under there. The human file was not edited.
- **`[risky]`** marks a concept that leans on an assumption the stories do not support. The assumption is named in the unit "Why the risky concepts are marked".
- **`[no app]`** marks the concepts for #8 that work without an app, since P6 uses none.
- **Nothing is ranked.** The order inside each table follows the directions, not any judgement of value.
- Ticketing, prices and subscriptions are out of the brief's scope; concepts that answered only a ticketing need were dropped.

## Directions

Eleven directions, each a different family of answers. Letters are labels, not an order; F is skipped (see below).

- **A · The service speaks first.** The service tells the person, unasked, at a moment it chooses. 1.1, 2.1, 4.1, 4.2, 7.1, 8.12.
- **B · See for yourself.** The state, its history or its likelihood is made visible for the person to judge. 1.5, 1.6, 2.2, 2.3, 4.3, 4.4, 7.2, 7.3, 8.2, 8.3, 8.4.
- **C · A person knows.** The answer comes from a person: staff, other riders. 1.7, 2.5, 4.10, 7.9, 7.10, 8.10.
- **D · Speak in decisions.** The person sets a rule, a train or a plan once, and the service answers in terms of it. 1.3, 1.4, 2.10, 4.5, 7.4, 7.5, 7.6.
- **E · A way out comes with the news.** The information arrives with what can be done instead. 1.11, 2.9, 4.8, 8.9.
- **G · Put a time on the unknown.** When the answer is not known, the service commits to when it will be. 1.9, 2.4, 4.11, 7.7, 8.6.
- **H · Lay out the futures.** Possible outcomes are shown as what each means for the person's trip. 1.10, 2.7, 2.8, 4.7, 7.8, 8.7, 8.8.
- **I · Say why.** The cause is given, not only the status. 1.8, 2.6, 8.1.
- **J · The place speaks.** The station carries the message to whoever is there. 2.11, 4.9, 7.11, 8.5.
- **K · Fold it into the routine.** The news lands inside something the person already does or keeps: their morning, their days ahead. 1.2, 4.6.
- **L · Change the service, not the message.** The operator changes how it decides or publishes, so there is less unknown to tell. 1.12, 2.12, 4.12, 7.12, 8.11.

Letter F (take the cost off the choice) from the previous version is not used: its concepts there rested on tickets, which are out of scope here.

## Why the risky concepts are marked

Each `[risky]` concept leans on one assumption the stories do not support.

- **1.6 · How likely this morning.** Assumes a delay or cancellation can be forecast before six. F1 only says the train builds delay on its way from Bassano.
- **1.7 · Word from earlier stops.** Assumes riders are at earlier stops before six and willing to report. F1 has P2 alone, checking at home.
- **1.12 · Settled before six.** Assumes the first train's fate can be known that early. F1 says the delay builds on the way, after P2 would leave.
- **2.3 · What usually happens next.** Assumes past days predict this one. F3 records one moment, not a pattern.
- **2.12 · A limbo that cannot last.** Assumes the operator could decide sooner than it does. F3 says nothing about why the state stays open.
- **4.6 · The strike in the student's days.** Assumes the student keeps a record of coming days the service could reach. F4 does not say so.
- **4.8 · An evening that fits.** Assumes the service knows when the student's lessons end. F4 only says P4 took the guaranteed train.
- **4.12 · The list as soon as the strike is called.** Assumes the guaranteed trains are known on the day the strike is announced. F4 does not say when P4's train was known to be guaranteed.
- **7.9 · Word from earlier stops.** Assumes riders upstream report the train. F8 has P3 reading the operator's own passage times.
- **7.10 · A person on the line.** Assumes someone from the service is reachable and knows more than the passage times. F8 gives no evidence of that.
- **8.4 · What usually happens next.** Assumes past evenings predict this one. F9 records one evening.
- **8.7 · A delay with a range.** Assumes a delay can be bounded while it is still growing. F9 gives no evidence of that.
- **8.10 · Ask at the station.** Assumes P6 would ask. The features note records that P6 knew the bus option and did not ask at the station.
- **8.11 · The delay told whole.** Assumes the full length is known when the delay starts. F9 is about not knowing it.
- **8.12 · A text about my usual train.** Assumes P6 would give a phone number to the service. F9 says only that P6 uses no app.

## Signals

This unit is my reading of the concepts, not what the sources say.

- **The human list covers A, B, D and J well.** Its matches fall mostly in telling, showing, and setting a rule once. Directions G (put a time on the unknown), H (lay out the futures) and L (change the service) have few or no matches in the human list.
- **L is new in this version.** It answers the questions by shrinking the unknown at the operator rather than reporting it better; it is also where most of the risk sits, since the stories say nothing about how the operator decides.
- **#7 is the narrowest question.** Its concepts crowd into B and D, as the HMW note predicted ([narrow], thin "so that").
- **#8 holds without an app.** Eleven of twelve concepts work from the board, the announcements or a person, matching P6's habit.
