# Clusters · 60 concepts in thirteen families, before boarding

## Scope and method

This note groups every concept already written for five How-Might-We questions into families of answers. Nothing new is invented: every row comes from `2026-10-26-02-ideation-human.md` or `2026-10-26-02.1-ideation-ai.md`. Neither input file was edited.

The chosen moment is before boarding: from days ahead up to the platform, when the commuter needs to know whether their train is late, cancelled or hit by a strike, and decide what to do.

The five questions:

- **#1 · P2.** An early commuter, before leaving home at six, learns that the first train is already late or cancelled.
- **#2 · P2.** An office commuter learns, as soon as it is decided, whether a train in limbo is cancelled or only late.
- **#4 · P4.** An evening student learns from the service, days ahead, that a strike hits the route and which trains still run.
- **#7 · P3.** A pass holder learns, before going to the station, when their own train will reach their stop.
- **#8 · P6.** A student at the station judges whether a delay will pass or grow, while there is still time to find another way.

How the merged list was built:

- **One row per concept.** Rows are numbered 1 to 60, grouped by question in the order #1, #2, #4, #7, #8. Inside each question the agent's order comes first, then the human concepts with no agent twin.
- **Author.** `[agent]` only in the agent file; `[me]` only in the human file; `[both]` an agent concept marked `[also mine]`, merged with its human twin into one row.
- **Wording.** A `[both]` row gives the agent name, then the human name after "·", then the agent's one line. A `[me]` row gives the human name and line.
- **Two agent concepts on one human twin.** The agent marked both 1.4 and 7.1 as the human "Leave-home reminder", and both 1.7 and 7.9 as the human "Reports from earlier stops". Each trio is one row, answering two questions.
- **The human "(yours)" marks.** The human file marks four concepts as taken from an earlier agent list. Three (Instant delay push, Delay and cancellation odds, Strikes section) have an `[also mine]` twin in the current agent file and are `[both]`. "Strike email with train table" has none, so it is `[me]` here.
- **`[risky]` and `[no app]`** are kept exactly where the agent file put them. The human file carries neither mark.

How the families were built:

- **A family is a direction:** concepts belong together when they answer the question in the same way, whatever the question, the wording or the channel. A push, an email and a station poster that give the same answer sit in one family.
- **Built from the concepts.** The agent file's directions A to L were not reused as a frame; a family that coincides with one does so because the concepts led there.
- **Not ranked.** Families are numbered F1 to F13 for reference only. No family is recommended.

Where a concept sat on an edge, the deciding line:

- **24, 34 (F1), not a station family.** The platform showing the decided state and the poster announcing the strike give the same answer as a push or an email: you are told without asking. The station is their channel, not their direction.
- **43 (F1), not F8.** Being told only on change is a way of telling unasked; the commuter decides nothing in advance. Row 3 is in F8 because P2 sets the threshold that triggers it.
- **47, 51 (F2).** A board read from home and a board showing the last station passed give the current state to look at, like rows 5 and 39.
- **50, 53 (F3), not F2.** The trend arrow and the announcement with outlook say where the delay is heading, not only where it stands.
- **45, 55 (F3), not F6.** A time with its certainty and a range are estimates of the outcome; they are not restated in terms of the commuter's trip.
- **21, 56 (F6), not F9.** They give the time left before the other ways close, not the other way itself.
- **18 (F12), not F11.** Staff confirm the state into the system for everyone; the commuter does not ask them.
