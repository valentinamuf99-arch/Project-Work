# Ideation · twelve concepts per question

## Scope and method

This note widens the range of answers to four How-Might-We questions taken from `2026-10-26-how-might-we-ai.md`: #1 (F1 · P2), #3 (F3 · P2), #4 (F4 · P4) and #9 (F11 · P1). It replaces the earlier version of this file, which was not read, so none of its concepts carry over.

- **Twelve concepts per question**, numbered 1 to 48 across the note. Each names the question it answers.
- **A direction** is a family of answers: a different way of answering, not a variation of one answer. The same direction can recur across questions; the eleven directions are listed in the Directions unit.
- **One line per concept**, saying what it does, not how. No channels, screens or features beyond what the concept needs to be told apart.
- **`[also mine]`** marks a concept that gives the same answer as one in `2026-10-26-ideation-human.md`, whatever the wording. The human file was not edited.
- **`[risky]`** marks a concept that leans on an assumption the stories do not support. The assumption is named in the unit "Why the risky concepts are marked".
- **Nothing is ranked.** The order inside each table follows the directions, not any judgement of value.
- Participants are cited by code only, as in the stories.

## Directions

Eleven directions, each a different family of answers. Letters are labels, not an order.

- **A · The service speaks first.** The service tells the person, unasked, at a moment it chooses. Concepts 1, 2, 25, 26, 36, 39.
- **B · See for yourself.** The state, its likelihood or its history is made visible for the person to judge. Concepts 3, 4, 5, 23, 27, 31, 40.
- **C · A person knows.** The answer comes from a person: staff, other riders, other passengers. Concepts 6, 20, 21, 33, 41, 47.
- **D · Speak in decisions.** The person sets a rule or a plan once, and the service answers in terms of that decision, not raw data. Concepts 7, 8, 24, 35, 46.
- **E · A way out comes with the news.** The information arrives together with what can be done instead. Concepts 9, 10, 17, 44.
- **F · Take the cost off the choice.** The service lowers what a wrong choice would cost, so deciding under doubt is safe. Concepts 18, 19.
- **G · Put a time on the unknown.** When the answer is not known, the service commits to when it will be. Concepts 12, 13, 34, 42.
- **H · Lay out the futures.** The possible outcomes are shown as what each means for the person's trip. Concepts 14, 15, 16, 45, 48.
- **I · Say why.** The cause is given, not only the status, so the person can judge what comes next. Concepts 11, 22, 43.
- **J · The place speaks.** The train or the station itself carries the message to whoever is there. Concepts 29, 32, 37, 38.
- **K · Build it into the days ahead.** The disruption is placed among the person's own coming days, ready to plan around. Concepts 28, 30.

Directions per question: #1 seven (A, B, C, D, E, G, I) · #3 eight (B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I) · #4 seven (A, B, C, D, G, J, K) · #9 nine (A, B, C, D, E, G, H, I, J).

## Why the risky concepts are marked

Each `[risky]` concept leans on one assumption the stories do not support.

- **4 · How likely this morning.** Assumes a delay or cancellation can be forecast before six from what is known then. F1 only says the train builds delay on its way from Bassano.
- **5 · The evening-before outlook.** Assumes the night before carries any signal about the next morning's first train. Nothing in F1 does.
- **6 · Word from earlier stops.** Assumes riders are already at earlier stops before six and willing to report. F1 has P2 alone, checking at home.
- **18 · A fallback kept ready.** Assumes the fallback could be lost if not kept, for example by running out. F3 says nothing about the alternatives being scarce.
- **19 · Any train counts.** Assumes the ticket limits P2's plan. F3 does not mention tickets; the problem it names is not knowing.
- **20 · Someone who knows the train.** Assumes a person from the service knows more than the live tracking shows. F3 gives no evidence of that.
- **21 · What the others chose.** Assumes other passengers know more, or choose better, than P2. No story supports it.
- **23 · What usually happens next.** Assumes past days predict this one. F3 records one moment, not a pattern.
- **28 · The strike in the student's week.** Assumes the student keeps a record of their coming days the service could reach. F4 does not say so.
- **30 · An evening that fits.** Assumes the service knows when the student's lessons end. F4 only says P4 took the guaranteed train.
- **37 · The speaker says it.** Assumes the gap is the absence of an announcement. Caveat ⚠C2 cuts against it: P5 says on-board announcements do give a restart time.
- **47 · Passengers on the same train.** Assumes fellow passengers know more than P1. F11 has P1 not knowing anything, with no sign that others did.
- **48 · The worst case.** Assumes the length of a stop can be bounded while the train is stopped. F11 gives no evidence of that.

## Signals

This unit is my reading of the concepts, not what the sources say.

- **The human list leans on telling and showing.** Its 17 matches fall mostly in A (the service speaks first), B (see for yourself) and J (the place speaks), with #3 reaching into C, E, F and H. Directions D (speak in decisions), G (put a time on the unknown), I (say why) and K (build it into the days ahead) have no match in the human list.
- **G and I recur across all the questions they could reach.** Saying when the answer will come, and saying why, appear in #1, #3 and #9. Both answer the uncertainty in the stories rather than the delay itself.
- **#4's wording narrows the range.** "From the service" rules out people outside it, which is why two concepts were dropped there and C has a single entry.
- **Risk clusters in #3.** Five of its twelve concepts are risky, mostly because F3 records one moment of not knowing and little about tickets, seats or other passengers.
- **⚠C2 touches the most obvious answer to #9.** The two most direct concepts (37, 38) are the ones the caveat questions; the others do not depend on announcements being absent.
