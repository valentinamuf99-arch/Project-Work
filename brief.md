# Brief 01 · Redesigning the commute

Master project, individual. This brief is the shared starting point for the research and for any AI-assisted work on the project. It describes a problem, not a solution.

## Context

- **Area:** Vicenza–Padova (Veneto), a corridor served by regional trains.
- **Who travels it:** people who commute by train to work or university, several days a week, on a route they know well.
- **Research window:** one week, 05/10 → 12/10/2026. Details in `plan.md`.

## Who is the user?

A regular train commuter who travels the same route between home and work or university. They know the route well: the usual train, the usual platform, the usual delays. Over time they have built a routine that works on ordinary days and that they no longer think about.

What we do not know yet, and want to learn from the interviews:

- which apps and sources they actually use, and when (hypothesis)
- how much flexibility they have if they arrive late (hypothesis)
- whether work and university commuters behave differently (hypothesis)

## What is the core problem?

They cannot tell when today is different.

On most days a delay of a few minutes is normal and needs no action. But sometimes the routine breaks: a cancelled train, a strike, a long delay that makes them miss a connection. When that happens, the commuter often realises it late, gets information in pieces from different sources, and has to decide what to do with partial information, often while already waiting on the platform.

The problem is therefore not only the disruption itself, but three moments around it (all hypotheses to verify):

1. **Noticing:** telling an ordinary delay from a break in the routine, and how late that happens.
2. **Understanding:** finding out what is going on and how long it will last, from sources that may disagree.
3. **Deciding:** choosing between waiting, changing route or giving up, often with alternatives found by chance.

## Why does this problem interest me?

I once waited an hour in Rome for a bus that never came, and I found a way home only by chance. I realised that the hardest part was not the delay but having to decide with no information.

This comes from **a single personal experience**, on a bus and in a different city. The research has to check whether it applies to train commuters in the Vicenza–Padova area, and whether the lack of information is really worse than the delay itself.

## Scope

**In scope**
- The day of a disrupted journey, from the moment something starts to go wrong to the moment the commuter reaches their destination or gives up.
- How commuters notice, understand and decide during that moment.
- The information sources they use today, official and unofficial.

**Out of scope (for now)**
- Ticketing, prices and subscriptions.
- Journey planning for trips the user does not make regularly.
- Improving the service itself (punctuality, frequency).
- Any design solution: this phase is research only.

## Assumptions

All the assumptions behind this brief are listed as A1–A11 in `questions.md`, section "Assumptions in the brief". A1–A9 come from the brief, while A10 and A11 are implicit in the project framing. They are treated as **hypotheses** until the research confirms or rejects them. The ones the research must test first are in `plan.md`, section 2.

## Key terms

- **Ordinary delay:** a delay the commuter expects and accepts without changing plans.
- **Break in the routine:** an event that forces the commuter to make a decision they would not normally make.
- **Alternative:** any other way to complete or give up the journey (another train, a bus leg, a lift, staying home).

## How to use this brief

- Treat everything here as a starting point, not as a finding.
- Do not propose solutions, features or technology unless explicitly asked.
- When something is assumed and not supported by evidence, say so.
- If the research contradicts this brief, the research wins: flag the conflict so the brief can be updated.
