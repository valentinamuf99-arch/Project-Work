# Interview script · Redesigning the commute

Semi-structured interview for the research defined in [plan.md](plan.md).
**Duration:** 30 min · **Format:** remote, video call, recorded with consent · **Participants:** commuters in the Vicenza–Padova area who travel by train to work or university (P1–P5).

## Before you start

- **Ask about real events.** Ask about things that actually happened, not about what they "usually" or "would" do. When an answer drifts into generalities, bring it back to a specific day: *"Tell me about a specific time."*
- **Don't introduce the brief's words.** Do not say "uncertainty", "information", "prediction" or "AI" before the participant does. Use their words in follow-ups.
- **Stay neutral.** Do not suggest answers, judge choices or describe possible solutions.
- **Key questions are in bold.** Each key question has one probe, marked with →. If time runs short, protect section 4.

## 1. Intro · 2 min

> Thank you for taking the time. I'm doing a university project on daily commuting in the Vicenza–Padova area. I'd like to understand how your journeys actually go, both normal days and the days when something goes wrong.
>
> There are no right or wrong answers. I'm not testing you or any app; I'm interested in your experience. You can skip any question and stop at any time.
>
> Is it OK for me to record this call? The recording is only for my notes. It won't be shared, and I'll refer to you with a code, never by name.

Start recording only after verbal consent. Repeat the consent question on the recording.

---

## 2. Warm-up · 2 min

1. Tell me a little about yourself and your commute: from where to where, how you get there, and how many days a week.

---

## 3. The routine · 4 min

2. **Think about the last normal day you commuted. Which apps or other sources did you use, and at what point in the journey?** (Q6, Q11)
   → How do you know that a day is going normally?

---

## 4. When the routine breaks · 12 min

3. **Tell me about the last time something went wrong with your commute: a delay, a cancellation, a strike, a missed connection, anything. What happened?**
   → Where were you when it started?
4. **When did you first realise it wasn't a normal day, and what told you?** (Q12, Q17)
   → Which app or source did you open, if any, and what did it say?
5. **What did you decide to do, and how did you come up with that option?** (Q24)
   → How long did you wait before acting, and what made you move? (Q11)
6. **Looking back at that day, what was the hardest part?** (Q15)
   → How did you feel while it was happening?
7. In the last month, how many times has something like this happened? (Q9)

---

## 5. Information and tools · 5 min

8. **Going back to that day, what did you want to know that you couldn't find out?** (Q17, Q22)
   → Which sources do you trust for your commute, and which have let you down?

---

## 6. Attitude towards help · 3 min

*Do not describe any solution and do not mention technology first.*

9. **Tell me about a time someone or something warned you about a problem, or suggested what to do, during your commute. How did you react?** (Q24, Q15)
   → Which decisions on your commute do you prefer to make yourself?

If the participant has no such episode, ask: *"Who do you turn to when something goes wrong on your commute?"*

---

## 7. Wrap-up · 2 min

10. What else about your commute do you think is important that we haven't talked about?

> Thank you, this has been really helpful. If you know someone else who commutes in the area and might like to talk to me, I'd be grateful for an introduction.

Stop the recording. Write the notes the same day in `research/interviews/P#.md`.
