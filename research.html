User stories · one story per feature

Scope and method

Each feature in 2026-10-23-features-ai.md was turned into at most one story in the form: As a [who], in [circumstance], I want [capability], so that [value].

  • Who is one interview participant, described by their commute and cited by code. App Store reviewers are not participants, so they cannot be the "who".
  • Circumstance is the moment in that participant's note, not the setting.
  • Capability is the feature's own "what it does" line, narrowed where the participant's moment is narrower, never widened.
  • Value traces to a quote, or to an observation in the note, marked as such.

A feature whose value could not be traced to a participant's quote or observation did not become a story. Those features are listed under "Back on the list" and marked [no story] in the features note.

No feature was split: in each story the capability answers one moment of one participant.

Each story is followed by its source line: feature · note · quote. Notes are cited by path from research/; codes as in the features note.

Research note structure — instructions for AI agents

This file is the format specification for this research repository. Any AI agent — or human — creating or editing a note here follows it exactly. Consistency between notes is what makes the knowledge base retrievable later: the same fields, in the same order, in every file.

The metadata block

Every note must open with a YAML frontmatter block, before any content:

---
title: "Descriptive, specific — one line"
date: 2026-10-08            # when the evidence was collected (YYYY-MM-DD)
time: "07:55–08:20"         # optional — time of day for interviews and observations
activity: interview         # interview | observation | shadowing | mystery-shopping | desk-research | plan
source: "Who or what produced the evidence — a person and their role, or a publication"
author: "Who collected or produced the note"
channel: in-person          # in-person | phone | video | web | publication
method: semi-structured interview   # the specific method used
location: Milano Centrale   # where — omit for desk research
tags: [commute, delays]     # lowercase, consistent vocabulary, no invention
confidence: high            # high | medium | low — how much you trust this evidence
grade: primary              # primary | secondary | tertiary — the source grade
status: complete            # draft | complete | needs-review
related: [plan.md]          # links to related notes in this repository
---

Field rules:

  • confidence is about this piece of evidence, not the topic. A low-confidence note is an honest note — mark it, don't hide it.
  • grade follows the source ladder: primary (you collected it, or it is original data) > secondary (someone's reporting of data) > tertiary (opinion, blog, unverified).
  • tags come from the research plan's tag list whenever possible. Do not invent near-synonyms (delay vs delays) — one term, one tag.
  • date is the collection date, not the writing date. The writing history is in git.

File naming

YYYY-MM-DD-type-source.md

Example: 2026-10-08-interview-marta-bianchi.md — lowercase, hyphen-separated. The filename alone says when it was collected, what kind of material it is, and where it came from.

Body structure

The body is plain prose organized into units: each ## heading is one unit, and one unit is one complete meaning. Rules:

  1. One unit, one meaning. If a unit covers two ideas, split it into two units.
  2. Self-contained sentences. Anyone — human or agent — reading only this note must understand it without opening other files.
  3. Quotes carry timestamps. In interview and observation notes, quotes are blockquotes with the timestamp from the transcript: > "..." — [00:04:12].
  4. No interpretation mixed with evidence. A separate final unit (## Signals) maps what was said to the brief's pain points — clearly marked as reading, not as what the person said.

Folders

research/
  plan.md          ← the research plan — the container everything answers to
  index.md         ← the map: every note listed with type and status
  notes/           ← derived, structured notes (one file per source)
  transcripts/     ← verbatim transcripts — artifacts, never edited
  raw/             ← unprocessed material: recordings, spontaneous notes, screenshots

Non-negotiable rules

  1. One file per source. Never merge, never rewrite. New evidence is a new note.
  2. Never edit a transcript. The transcript is the artifact; the note is the derived view. If a finding is questioned later, you go back to the transcript.
  3. Every note lands in index.md the moment it is committed, with type and status.
  4. Update related links whenever a note connects to another — the network is the scalability.
  5. Same metadata at 10 notes and at 200. Nothing about the shape changes as the base grows.

Interview script · Redesigning the commute

Semi-structured interview for the research defined in research/plan.md. Duration: 30 min · Format: remote, video call, recorded with consent · Participants: commuters in the Vicenza–Padova area who travel by train to work or university (P1–P5).

Before you start

  • Ask about real events. Ask about things that actually happened, not about what they "usually" or "would" do. When an answer drifts into generalities, bring it back to a specific day: "Tell me about a specific time."
  • Don't introduce the brief's words. Do not say "uncertainty", "information", "prediction" or "AI" before the participant does. Use their words in follow-ups.
  • Stay neutral. Do not suggest answers, judge choices or describe possible solutions.
  • Key questions are in bold. Each key question has one probe, marked with →. If time runs short, protect section 4.

I'd like to understand how your journeys actually go, both normal days and the days when something goes wrong. There are no right or wrong answers. I'm not testing you or any app; I'm interested in your experience. You can skip any question and stop at any time. Is it OK for me to record this call? The recording is only for my notes. It won't be shared, and I'll refer to you with a code, never by name.

Start recording only after verbal consent. Repeat the consent question on the recording.

2. Warm-up · 2 min

  1. Tell me a little about yourself and your commute: from where to where, how you get there, and how many days a week.

3. The routine · 4 min

  1. Think about the last normal day you commuted. Which apps or other sources did you use, and at what point in the journey? (Q6, Q11)

→ How do you know that a day is going normally?

4. When the routine breaks · 12 min

  1. Tell me about the last time something went wrong with your commute: a delay, a cancellation, a strike, a missed connection, anything. What happened?

→ Where were you when it started?

  1. When did you first realise it wasn't a normal day, and what told you? (Q12, Q17)

→ Which app or source did you open, if any, and what did it say?

  1. What did you decide to do, and how did you come up with that option? (Q24)

→ How long did you wait before acting, and what made you move? (Q11)

  1. Looking back at that day, what was the hardest part? (Q15)

→ How did you feel while it was happening?

  1. In the last month, how many times has something like this happened? (Q9)

5. Information and tools · 5 min

  1. Going back to that day, what did you want to know that you couldn't find out? (Q17, Q22)

→ Which sources do you trust for your commute, and which have let you down?

6. Attitude towards help · 3 min

Do not describe any solution and do not mention technology first.

  1. Tell me about a time someone or something warned you about a problem, or suggested what to do, during your commute. How did you react? (Q24, Q15)

→ Which decisions on your commute do you prefer to make yourself?

If the participant has no such episode, ask: "Who do you turn to when something goes wrong on your commute?"

7. Wrap-up · 2 min

  1. What else about your commute do you think is important that we haven't talked about?

Thank you, this has been really helpful. If you know someone else who commutes in the area and might like to talk to me, I'd be grateful for an introduction.

Desk research · benchmark of the apps participants mention

Scope and method

This benchmark covers the five apps that came up in the interviews: Trenitalia (app and website), ViaggiaTreno, Trainline, AVM Venezia and SVT Vicenza. Google Maps, mentioned by P2 for bus alternatives, appears only in the comparison table. For each app it records the core commuting features, how it handles disruptions (real-time information, delay causes, notifications, alternatives) and its main UX strengths and weaknesses.

Evidence comes from official store listings and operator pages, trade press and a 2023 document of the transport regulator (ART), read on 2026-10-12. What participants said is reported from the interview notes, with the participant code. The plan asks for the benchmark to be verified on a phone: that was not done for this note. The note is closed as it is: every point marked "unverified" remains untested, and the checks are listed below as open work for later.

Trenitalia: official tickets, notifications that arrive late

All six participants mention Trenitalia, mostly to buy tickets and passes (P2, P3, P5). P6 buys the pass on the website and does not use the app.

  • Commuting features: single tickets and regional passes, journey search, an "Infomobilità" section, and Smart Caring, a free service that follows up to four trains by number and sends push notifications.
  • Disruptions, as published: Smart Caring sends the departure platform, delays and line problems with the reason. Since 15 May 2024, holders of a regional digital ticket also receive information on cancellations, delays and rerouting for the train they are on.
  • Disruptions, as experienced: the email arrives late or about two minutes before the on-board announcement (P1, P3); a cancelled train is shown as cancelled only long after, so a delay cannot be told from a cancellation (P2); a line fault produced no notice at all (P4); strikes appear on the website but not in the app (P4); the network-wide banner is not filtered to the user's journey and is ignored (P2).
  • UX strengths: the official source, linked to the ticket and the pass; following a train by number is supported.
  • UX weaknesses: a gap between what the service promises and what participants live; ticket sales close a few minutes before departure (P2, P5); refund and rebooking rules require a Google search every time (P2); P6 met bugs once and stopped using the app.
  • Unverified: whether Smart Caring is active on every Veneto regional line, and whether pass holders receive it without setting it up. No participant mentioned it.

ViaggiaTreno: accurate real-time running, found by word of mouth

Only P3 uses ViaggiaTreno. She heard of it from a fellow student and trusts it more than the Trenitalia app for real-time status.

  • Commuting features: search by train number, with actual arrival and departure times stop by stop, and station departure and arrival boards. No tickets.
  • Disruptions: strong on real-time running ("almost always accurate", P3). No notifications, no alternatives, and the cause of a delay is rarely detailed.
  • UX strengths: accurate, fast, no account needed.
  • UX weaknesses: a dated website ("poorly made", P3); the user must know the train number and check it actively; almost nobody knows it exists.
  • Unverified: this section relies on prior knowledge of the site, not on a check made for this note, including whether an official mobile app still exists.

Trainline: the most polished interface, train only

Only P5 uses Trainline, for single same-day tickets, because she finds it more convenient.

  • Commuting features: tickets from several operators (Trenitalia and Italo) and "Orari Live", real-time status of Trenitalia and Italo trains with platform, delays and cancellations, a live tracker of the train's position, and search by station or train number.
  • Disruptions: delay and cancellation alerts before and during the journey, notifications for booked trips. A 2023 ART decision records Trenitalia's commitment to extend push information for regional passengers, including replacement buses and alternative routes, to tickets bought through platforms such as Trainline and Omio.
  • UX strengths: the cleanest interface of the five, with real-time information and the ticket in the same place.
  • UX weaknesses: no buses or boats.
  • Unverified: whether Trainline sells Veneto regional passes (probably not, which would leave pass holders without ticket-linked information), and whether the regional push information committed in 2023 is live today.

AVM Venezia: a ticket wallet for Venice

Only P1 uses AVM Venezia, for boat tickets linked to her resident card.

  • Commuting features: purchase and validation of AVM/Actv tickets by QR code, a trip planner, timetables by stop, mobility news, payment for parking.
  • Disruptions: general news only. The store listing mentions neither real-time arrivals nor push notifications.
  • UX strengths: linked to the resident card; covers several Venice modes (bus, tram, boat).
  • UX weaknesses: rated 1.2 out of 5 on the App Store (173 ratings); a user report says some vehicles have no QR reader and accept only RFID cards (the report is undated).
  • Unverified: real-time information and notifications.

SVT: tickets on the phone, no information found

Only P1 uses SVT Vicenza, for bus tickets.

  • Commuting features: ticket purchase in the SVT Vicenza app. A second app, SVT ChiamaBus, books the evening on-demand service in the Vicenza urban area.
  • Disruptions: no evidence found of real-time information, delay causes or notifications.
  • UX strengths: the ticket is on the phone.
  • UX weaknesses: a ticket tool, not an information tool, split across two apps. P2 says bus apps in general are much worse than train apps.
  • Unverified: real-time information and notifications.

Gaps no app covers well

  • Noticing: no app tells an ordinary delay from a break early enough. A delay and a cancellation look the same until it is too late (P1, P2, P4).
  • Multi-operator journeys: a boat, a train and a bus mean three apps and three tickets (P1). No app knows that a train delay breaks the bus connection.
  • Deciding: no app proposes an alternative in the moment together with the rules, for example whether the pass is valid on it. Replacement buses did not accept the pass (P3); rebooking rules need a Google search (P2).
  • Strikes: participants learn of them from Google, Instagram, Telegram or the conductor (P2, P4, P5), not linked to their own journey.
  • Understanding: the cause and the expected duration come from people (staff, conductor), not from apps (P1, P4, P6).
  • People outside the apps: P6 uses no app at all; P2, P3 and P5 open one only to buy tickets and otherwise rely on the board.

Opportunities for the concept

  • One personal alert with no setup: learn the usual journey from the pass or from habits, stay silent on ordinary days, speak only when the routine is at risk ("only my journeys, not spam", P2) and before leaving home (P2, P5).
  • A break detector: combine real-time running data, of the kind ViaggiaTreno shows, with the user's connections, to say "you will miss the Mestre connection" instead of "+12 min".
  • A "what are my options" card: wait, change route or give up, each with whether the ticket or pass is valid, including replacement buses and nearby stations. The decision stays with the user.
  • Strikes on the user's own calendar: which of their usual trains are guaranteed and which are not.
  • Notifying others: a ready message for an employer, a lecturer or family (P4 called her parents four times in one month).
  • A channel outside the app for people like P6: SMS or the station itself.
  • Dependency: all of this needs open data (GTFS, GTFS Realtime) for Trenitalia, SVT and Busitalia Veneto, which has not been checked yet.

Contradictions with the interview notes

  • Contradiction: the "People outside the apps" bullet above says P2 and P3 open an app only to buy tickets. The P2 note records a delay check in the app before leaving home and a search for alternative trains in it; the P3 note records a check in the app or website before leaving and ViaggiaTreno for real-time status.
  • Contradiction: the "Understanding" bullet above cites P1 for causes and durations coming from people. The P1 note records that no staff were available and that she never learned the reason.

Desk research · App Store reviews of Trenitalia and Trainline

Scope and method

Trenitalia is the app participants mention most (all six, P1 to P6). Trainline is mentioned only by P5, but it is the only other train app in the interviews, so it is included as a comparison. On 2026-10-13 the "most recent" reviews of both apps were read on the Italian App Store and captured as screenshots: 21 for Trenitalia (research/raw/Trenitalia screenshot reviews/, IMG_2375 to IMG_2395) and 12 for Trainline (research/raw/Trainline screenshot reviews/, IMG_2396 to IMG_2407). That is roughly 45 Trenitalia and 30 Trainline reviews, mostly dated May to October 2026, many with a reply from the developer.

The reviews were read in full and grouped by theme. Quotes are verbatim in Italian, followed by an English gloss, and cite the screenshot file instead of a timestamp. Reviewer usernames are not reported: reviewers are not participants, and some handles look like real names.

Limits of this evidence

  • Self-selection: people mostly write a review when something has gone wrong, so the sample leans negative. The few five-star reviews are short.
  • Unknown reviewers: there is no way to know who is a commuter, where they travel or whether the story is accurate. Some say they are commuters ("pendolare"); only one review names a Veneto line (Venezia–Trieste).
  • Partial screenshots: some reviews are cut at the top or bottom of a screenshot and are used only where the readable part is clear.
  • Use: this note can support or question patterns from the interviews and point to new ones to check. It does not carry the same weight as the interviews, which is why grade is secondary and confidence is medium.

Real-time information is wrong when it matters

Several reviews say the live status is unreliable. One commuter was sent to platform 11 at Bologna while the train left from the opposite side of the station, and the conductor advised not to rely on the app.

"Dopo aver chiesto al capotreno spiegazioni mi ha consigliato di non affidarmi all'applicazione per la consultazione." [IMG_2389] (the conductor told me not to rely on the app)

"Purtroppo l'aggiornamento in tempo reale è sempre sbagliato. Linea Venezia Trieste il personale dì bordo non aggiorna correttamente" [IMG_2383] (real-time updates are always wrong on the Venezia–Trieste line)

On Trainline, delays are reported as rarely shown, or shown when there is none [IMG_2401], and the train's position as wrong ("siamo a termini e dice che siamo a san gennaro", we are at Termini and it says San Gennaro) [IMG_2401]. One user's app stopped updating on the very train they were on, after a 33-minute delay that made them miss the change [IMG_2403].

Notifications are noise, and season-ticket holders get none for their own route

One Trenitalia user receives 8 to 10 identical morning notifications about night works, partly in English, and switched them off, losing the useful alerts about faults on the line too [IMG_2378].

Another, a pass holder, says single-ticket buyers get trip notifications but pass holders do not, even for cancellations and route changes on the route the pass covers. The consequence is concrete:

"Almeno una volta al mese mi ritrovo a piedi o se va bene arrivare a lavoro in ritardo - dovendo comunque prendere l'auto." [IMG_2386] (at least once a month I'm left on foot, or at best late for work, having to take the car anyway)

The commuter's main task is checking, not buying

Trainline replaced the Orario Treni app, and a large share of its recent reviews come from former Orario Treni users. They list what they lost: search by train number, a station board of arrivals and departures with platforms and delays, saved stations, saved recurring routes, and a single screen with the train's progress [IMG_2397, IMG_2399, IMG_2400, IMG_2406, IMG_2407].

"In generale l'esperienza d'uso è troppo incentrata sull'acquisto di un biglietto e poco sul verificare i treni disponibili o i loro eventuali ritardi" [IMG_2407] (the experience is too centred on buying a ticket and too little on checking trains and delays)

"Sono un pendolare, e ho bisogno di monitorare la situazione dei treni nella mia stazione per capire l'andamento del mio treno e quale mi convenga prendere." [IMG_2400] (I'm a commuter and need to monitor my station to see how my train is doing and which one to take)

The same users stress speed in a hurry: "quando si è di corsa (letteralmente) risulta difficile schiacciare ottocento cose" (when you are literally running it is hard to tap eight hundred things) [IMG_2396]. The same pattern appears for Trenitalia: one regular user finds the live train view "meno intuitiva e più dispersiva" (less intuitive, more scattered) after an update [IMG_2384].

Ticket rules ignore delays

Trenitalia closes ticket sales 5 minutes before the scheduled departure, even when the train is late. One user at the station at 19:00, with the 19:05 train 10 minutes late, could not buy a ticket for it and had to buy one for the next train, at the risk of a fine for boarding the earlier one [IMG_2376]. A second user reports the same rule and a fine received once [IMG_2392]. Trenitalia replies that the limit comes from regulation, not from the app.

After a cancellation, recovery is pushed onto the user

  • Refund and compensation: the "indennizzo" button returns "Informazione al momento non disponibile" for days, and the website form freezes on "motivo della richiesta" [IMG_2387]. A user who bought a ticket a few minutes before a cancellation found they had no right to a refund [IMG_2388]. Trenitalia's replies send users to the call center, a web form or the Smart Refund platform [IMG_2378, IMG_2387, IMG_2388].
  • Changing train: "gestisci biglietto" fails with "qualcosa è andato storto"; the user calls it "Funzione indispensabile per un pendolare" (essential for a commuter) [IMG_2377].
  • Missing ticket: a paid ticket never appeared; after five staff members and a 40-minute call center wait, the user paid twice [IMG_2393].
  • Trainline: the platform says compensation is handled by the rail company, the insurance sold with the ticket did not pay for missed connections, and a refund for a bus that never ran was refused because the operator refused it [IMG_2398, IMG_2402].

One review title sums it up: "Muro di gomma" (a rubber wall) [IMG_2375].

Connections and alternatives

  • Both apps propose tight connections. Trenitalia users ask why the app sells connections the trains cannot keep [IMG_2385] and suggest offering alternatives with wider connection times [IMG_2381]. A Trainline user is offered 5-minute connections on long routes, "che anche se i treni fossero in orario si farebbe fatica" (hard even if trains were on time) [IMG_2397].
  • After a missed connection, no alternative is offered: "non mi ha inviato neanche delle alternative di treni che avrei potuto prendere per tornare" [IMG_2403].
  • Replacement buses: one did not stop at the station where the ticket started [IMG_2379]; a Trainline user did not know where to catch one or which operator ran it [IMG_2404]. A route suspended for planned works in July and August was not flagged before purchase [IMG_2394].

For the user, the app is the service

Many one-star reviews are about delays, dirty trains and prices, not app features. Trenitalia's standard reply asks users to rate "solamente i servizi dell'app" (only the app's features) [IMG_2377, IMG_2379, IMG_2380, IMG_2385, IMG_2391]. Users do not make that distinction: the app is where they meet the service, so its failures are rated there.

"su 6 treni della giornata, 5 erano in ritardo di almeno mezz'ora, non usufruirò più del servizio Trenitalia" [IMG_2377] (5 of 6 trains today were at least half an hour late; I won't use Trenitalia again)

Updates break habits

"Peggiorata con aggiornamento" (worse after the update) is a recurring title for both apps. Regular users report buttons that do not respond, purchase flows that ask again for saved data, and a navigation they find more complicated [IMG_2383, IMG_2384, IMG_2390, IMG_2391]. A commuter works by habit, so every change to the layout has a cost.

What users value

The few positive reviews value having tickets always at hand, real-time updates when they work, a fast purchase [IMG_2388], and, for Trainline, an overview of all the travel options [IMG_2398].

Patterns confirmed by the interviews

  • Noticing: no warning, or a late one, before a break. Reviews: pass holders get no alerts [IMG_2386]. Interviews: an email two minutes before the announcement (P1), no notice of a line fault (P4), strikes never notified (P2), warnings only a few minutes ahead (P3).
  • Ticket sales closing before departure: also in the P2 and P5 notes. The reviews add the case where the train is late and the rule still applies.
  • Missed connections and no alternative proposed: the main break point for P1 and P2 (the change at Mestre). The reviews show the apps themselves sell tight connections.
  • Replacement buses that do not work as alternatives: also in the P3, P4 and P6 notes.
  • Trust in people over the app: a conductor tells a reviewer not to trust the app [IMG_2389]; in the interviews the conductor is often the one who explains (P3, P4).

New or stronger in the reviews

  • Pass holders as second-class users of notifications: worth asking P3, P5 and P6, who hold passes.
  • Checking vs buying: the strongest theme for Trainline. It matches the brief's moment before departure: a station board, a train by number, saved routes, one screen, fast.
  • Recovery after the break (refund, change of ticket, compensation) is a fourth moment the brief does not name, and it is where reviews are angriest.
  • Notification fatigue: generic alerts get switched off, and useful ones are lost with them. This supports P2's "only my journeys".

Opportunities for the concept

  • Alerts tied to the pass and the usual route, silent on ordinary days.
  • A commuter view built for checking, not for buying: my station, my train, my connection, in one screen.
  • When a connection is at risk, offer the next feasible option with wider margins, and say whether the ticket is valid on it.
  • After a cancellation, carry the user into the refund or change from the same place, without sending them to another channel.

Desk research · commuting in Veneto in numbers

Scope and method

This note sizes the problem the brief is about. It answers three questions with public figures: how many people commute in Veneto, how often regional trains are late or cancelled, and how much time is at stake.

Sources were read on 2026-10-13. Istat and Trenitalia PDFs were downloaded and their text extracted; figures are copied from the tables, with the table name. Where a figure is an estimate made for this note, it is labelled estimate and the calculation is shown.

grade is secondary although most figures are official: the punctuality figures are targets, not measured results, and the 2026 ridership figures reach this note through the press.

Limits of this evidence

  • Measured punctuality for Veneto is not public in readable form. Both Trenitalia service charters have a column "Consuntivo" (actual result), but it is empty in the extracted text of both PDFs. Only the targets ("Obiettivo") could be read. The actual figures may be in an image or simply not filled in; this was not resolved.
  • No official count of cancelled trains for Veneto was found. The charters do not report it, and the RFI booklet reports punctuality only.
  • No count of strike days for 2025 or 2026 was found from an official source (the Commissione di Garanzia calendar was not checked).
  • Official punctuality excludes strikes and "eventi eccezionali", which are exactly the breaks the brief is about. The figures describe the ordinary day, not the bad day.
  • Mode and travel time by region are last published for 2011. The 2019 and 2021 Istat releases give counts but not the train share for Veneto.
  • No source gives average minutes lost per delayed trip. Time lost is estimated below from thresholds, not measured.

How many commute: 2.7 million daily trips for work or study

Istat, permanent census 2019 (release of 13 May 2021, Prospetto 1): 2,690,173 Veneto residents travel every day to their place of study or work, 55.1% of the resident population, the fourth highest share in Italy (national 50.7%). Of these, 1,904,560 travel for work (70.8%) and 785,613 for study (29.2%).

1,455,575 of them (54.1%) leave their own municipality, one of the highest shares in Italy (national 42.5%).

For comparison, the 2011 census counted 2,603,830 daily commuters in Veneto (1,810,278 for work, 793,552 for study).

The 2021 origin-destination matrices for work (published 2 October 2025) and study (22 September 2026) exist in the Istat data browser, but their regional totals were not extracted for this note.

On the Venezia line, delays of 6 to 15 minutes and cancellations increased compared with the two previous years.

What the rules count as a break

The 2026 charter sets two thresholds that define, in practice, when the operator owes the commuter something:

  • Pass holders (section 8.4): compensation of 10% of the monthly pass for each month in which 10% or more of the trains on the route arrive more than 15 minutes late or are cancelled. Amounts under €4 are not paid.
  • Long delays (section 8.2): for a delay over 60 minutes, a missed connection or a cancellation, Trenitalia must communicate an alternative within 100 minutes of the scheduled departure; otherwise the passenger can claim the documented cost of reaching the destination by other public transport. Single tickets get 25% back for delays of 60 to 119 minutes and 50% from 120 minutes (tickets of €16 or more only).

Estimate: time at stake

No source gives average minutes of delay. A floor can be set from the thresholds: a trip more than 15 minutes late costs at least 15 minutes. With about 1.2 such trips a month at peak, a commuter loses at least 18 minutes a month to long delays on the ordinary day, on top of shorter delays. This is small against the 54-minute daily average, which supports what participants say: the ordinary delay is absorbed, and the cost sits in the rare break (a cancellation, a missed connection, a strike) that the official figures exclude. P2 measures a cancellation as up to an hour ("La cancellazione può significare un ritardo di un'ora se devi aspettare quello dopo"); P4's line fault lasted an hour and a half before the cause was known.

Signals

This unit is a reading of the figures above, not what the sources say.

  • The population is large, the train share small. About 2.7 million Veneto residents commute daily and over half leave their municipality, but in 2011 only about 2.6% used the train as their main mode. Train commuters are a minority inside a car region, which is why family cars are the fallback in the interviews (P2, P4, P6).
  • Participants travel on the region's least punctual lines. Venezia–Verona (Padova, Vicenza) and Venezia–Bologna (Padova, Monselice, Rovigo) have the lowest punctuality targets in Veneto, and the peak hours have lower targets than the rest of the day.

  • The breaks are invisible in the official numbers. Strikes and exceptional events are excluded from punctuality; cancellations are not reported. The events the brief is about are, by construction, outside the measurement. Their frequency has to come from the interviews (for example P4: four times in one month last semester) or from independent monitoring.
  • The compensation threshold is out of reach. A pass holder is compensated only in a month three times worse than the target, which may explain why no participant mentions it.

Who she is and what her commute looks like

P1 commutes to work from the Lido di Venezia to Torri di Quartesolo, just outside Vicenza. She chains three modes: the boat from the Lido to the mainland, the train to Vicenza and the bus from Vicenza to Torri di Quartesolo. She has worked there since 2021 and no longer goes to the office every day.

"Vado dal lido, quindi al lido di Venezia, a Torri Borzolo. Quindi fondamentalmente prendo la barca." — [00:03:11]

Without delays the door-to-door journey takes about two hours and forty minutes; with problems it takes longer.

"Allora, ci metto circa 2 ore e mezzo in totale, quasi 3, dipende. Allora, senza considerare eventuali ritardi, sono sulle 2 ore e 40, diciamo, più o meno." — [00:03:28]

A railway stop closer to Torri exists, but there is no bus from there to her office, so she always gets off at Vicenza.

"Cioè potrei fermarmi direttamente a Lerino col treno, il problema è che non c'è l'autobus per andare a. Ecco." — [00:04:09]

The connections are the weak point

For P1 the problem is not any single mode but the change from one to the next: every leg ends with a wait for the next one.

"Però tendenzialmente il problema sono le coincidenze, quindi per esempio una volta magari... Cioè arrivo col treno, devo aspettare la barca." — [00:03:52]

Three apps for three modes, used for tickets

All her tickets are on her phone, in three separate apps: AVM Venezia for the boat (linked to her resident card), Trenitalia for the train and SVT Vicenza for the bus.

"Allora, io utilizzo solo ed esclusivamente applicazioni per tutto. Okay. Perché tutti I biglietti sono del telefono." — [00:05:09]

"Si chiama AVM Venezia, che è l'applicazione fondamentalmente di di Venezia dove io ho messo la mia carta, la mia tessera da residente e ho la possibilità di comprare I biglietti per la barca." — [00:05:20]

"Ho uso l'app di Italia per comprare il treno. Treno. E poi per I biglietti dell'autobus utilizzo SVT Vicenza." — [00:05:40]

On an ordinary day she opens each app once, at the start of its leg, to validate the ticket. Trenitalia stays open only to show the ticket if a conductor checks it. She does not mention using any of the three for live information on an ordinary day.

"Allora le le le utilizzi di solito all'inizio per per timbro, cioè quindi per effettivamente convalidare il biglietto, a parte quella di Italia che la tengo solo a testa per far vedere il biglietto." — [00:06:02]

The station board, not the phone

At the station she reads the departure boards rather than her phone. She does not like keeping the phone out among other people.

"Guardo sempre il monitor perché non voglio tener fuori il telefono, mi dà fastidio" — [00:08:43]

"Cioè preferisco tenermi la mia roba in tasca." — [00:08:47]

How she notices that something is wrong

She has no single signal. On the boat and at the bus stop, the sign is a crowd: too many people waiting means a run has been skipped.

"Per esempio, allora tendenzialmente se vedo tanta gente, tanta tanta gente sulla barca o aspettare l'autobus capisco che c'è qualcosa che non va." — [00:07:03]

On the train, she notices only when it does not leave.

"Il treno dipende perché ecco capisco che magari il treno non... C'è qualcosa che non va quando sto fermo. Semplicemente salgo e e non parte." — [00:07:16]

The ordinary delay: the bus is always late

Bus delays do not count as a problem for her. She is used to them and plans around them by picking an earlier or later run.

"Allora, partiamo col presupposto che l'autobus tendenzialmente è sempre un po' in ritardo, quindi non è un problema, cioè sono abituata, so che devo prendere... Non posso prendere il treno 1º, devo prendere il treno dopo. Oppure l'autobus 1º" — [00:08:17]

The last disrupted journey: a board that said on time

On her worst recent day, on the way home, the station boards showed many trains delayed or cancelled, but not hers. She waited. Her train then arrived at least fifteen minutes late and crowded.

"E allora ho guardato I monitor e vedo 20 minuti, 30 minuti, cancellato. Any cosa? Cosa sta succedendo?" — [00:08:47]

"Il mio treno non sembra in ritardo, va bene, mi metto in... Mi metto a aspettare. Grandissima cazzata perché in realtà era era in ritardo, è di almeno 15 minuti" — [00:09:00]

Near Padova the train stopped, moved a little, and stopped again, with no explanation on board.

"Eravamo appunto a Padova e vediamo che il treno sta fermo. Va va un po' più avanti e poi si ferma di nuovo." — [00:10:02]

The cancellation arrives by email, without a reason

The only notice came by email, because she had bought the ticket in the app, about two minutes before the announcement on board. It said the train was cancelled and would terminate at Mestre. It gave no reason. Later in the interview she also mentions a message "to everyone" on the train saying only that it would stop at Mestre ([00:17:15]); the transcript does not make clear whether this is the same email or a separate on-board notice.

"L'unica cosa che mi fatto sapere è stato letteralmente 2 minuti 1º del... Dell'annuncio, mi è arrivata una mail." — [00:10:16]

"Perché ho ho comprato il biglietto e mi detto il treno è stato cancellato e si ferma a Mestre." — [00:10:33]

"dire il motivo. Io non sapevo le motivazioni, non sapevo niente, infatti lì per lì ho un attimo panicato perché ho detto che cazzo faccio, era era tardi." — [00:10:42]

While the train was stopped she opened the Trenitalia app to look for other trains, and messaged someone asking what to do: bus, another train, or something else.

"Io ho guardato... Ecco lì un altro motivo in cui ho guardato l'applicazione per vedere se c'erano dei treni che potevo prendere per andare..." — [00:07:47]

The alternative was found by chance

At Mestre everyone got off. She had given up and was heading for the bus when, walking through the underpass, she saw on a board a regional train to Venezia that had not left yet. Nobody had told her about it. She ran for it, afraid it would be cancelled too.

"Vado vado verso l'autobus perché non vedevo boh, non so, io non so se prendere un 1 treno o no, però non so cosa è successo, mentre ero nel sottopassaggio ho visto un treno, un regionale, permanenza, l'ho visto negli... Nei cartelloni fuori." — [00:11:03]

"Assolutamente no, no, no. È stato random e ho detto aspetta, ho guardato il suo Venezia, ho visto allora, visto che non è partito, ho visto gente che correva per andare." — [00:11:35]

"Avevo paura che magari il treno partisse perché appunto era già oltre l'orario, e e la mia paura era che bloccassero anche quello perché non non si sapevano" — [00:12:03]

One late leg breaks the next one

The train got her to Venezia, but late, and the boat to the Lido had just gone. She waited another half hour for the next one, on a boat that was again crowded.

"Però tardi e torno a dire lì, oltre a quello, mi mi sbagliato completamente tutto perché quando sono arrivata non c'era il battello. Mi dovuto aspettare mezz'ora per il battello." — [00:12:32]

The hardest part: being stranded at Mestre, alone, in the evening

The hardest part was not the delay itself but the prospect of being stuck at Mestre, a station she does not know well and does not feel safe in, and of taking a bus at a late hour on her own. Nobody could come to pick her up.

"Non proprio panico ma un po', non tanto per per... Sarei comunque tornato a casa in qualche modo, è più il il fatto che mi sarei fermata in una stazione che a me non piace per niente." — [00:13:32]

"E quindi l'idea di prendermi magari un autobus a un'ora che non è il top con non so chi persone, perché purtroppo maestri veramente è orrenda. Cioè a me... A me non è una cosa che mi fa... Mi tranquillizza, ero da sola." — [00:13:52]

Living on public transport: never knowing when she gets home

Living on the Lido, she depends entirely on public transport. One cancelled leg pushes her onto the evening boats, which run about once an hour. Her summary is that she never knows when she will get home.

"Ecco io la difficoltà che ho visto di più è che vivendo appunto al Lido di Venezia, io sono noi mezzi, cioè come ci torno a casa." — [00:14:16]

"Se mi cancellavano il treno e non trovavo un treno per tornare a casa, trovavo un autobus, tornavo a casa tardi, I battelli si diradano, ce ne ho tipo uno all'ora." — [00:14:39]

"Io non lo so, io ogni volta che prendo l'autobus o comunque quando vengo, io non so quando torno, non so quando arrivo. Cioè, io suppongo di arrivare così se va tutto bene, ma non lo so." — [00:14:56]

"Okay, cioè 6 in balia completamente della situazione" — [00:15:13]

How often it happens

Delays happen all the time. A break like this one happens two or three times a year, and this was the worst in recent memory.

"Non capita spesso perché, allora, in ritardi ce ne sono. Okay. Sempre, ma non così. Così capiterà 3... 2 3 volte l'anno." — [00:15:27]

"Sì, di questi ultimo tempo sì, è stato proprio il peggiore in assoluto infatti." — [00:15:44]

What she wanted to know: why, how long, where to go

She wanted three things: the reason for the stop (and why her train, when others were running), how long it would take, and above all where she could go next.

"Cioè perché mi blocchi il treno? Non non... E soprattutto non è l'unico che blocchi. Cioè scusa, è l'unico che blocchi gli altri vanno?" — [00:16:00]

"Cioè fammi capire qual è il problema, qual è la tempistica, perché anche quello, e fammi capire soprattutto dove cavolo posso andare." — [00:16:19]

"Io avrei voluto sapere quali altri treni ci sono che avrei potuto prendere e prendere Venezia." — [00:17:41]

She knows Mestre; she points out that a traveller who does not, such as a foreign tourist, would be completely lost.

"Io penso a uno straniero che magari per quel treno viene da Verona a se uno straniero fa da Verona a Venezia, arriva a a Mestre, dice che dove cazzo sono? Cosa devo fare?" — [00:16:45]

The official message offers options that do not help

The message she received listed generic options: continue on the first useful train, give up the journey, or ask for a refund. To her these are not real alternatives: giving up is not an option when the destination is home. She read it as the operator washing its hands of the problem.

"Il treno è... Oggi è limitato a Venezia Nestre. Può proseguire il 1º treno utile successivo, rinunciare al viaggio o chiedere rimborso." — [00:17:47]

"Rinuncerei al viaggio zio, se devo andare a casa. [...] È un po' un messaggio un po' messo lì come per dire vabbè." — [00:18:05]

"Io ti ho detto che devi fermarti, cioè non ti dico ci sono alternative, non ti dico niente." — [00:18:24]

She has never been warned or advised

Nobody and nothing has ever warned her of a problem in advance or suggested what to do during a journey.

"No no, non è mai successo, assolutamente." — [00:18:44]

Nobody to ask, so she becomes the one who is asked

There is no staff to turn to: since 2021 she has seen a train conductor about three times. She relies on the internet, and is often asked for help herself by passengers who do not read the boards.

"Anche se volessi parlare con magari un 1 controllore, io non non potrei perché non c'è. Cioè l'ho visto veramente in dal 2021 che lavoro a avrò visto il controllore del treno 3 volt." — [00:19:03]

"Cioè io se devo chiedere qualcosa a qualcuno cosa faccio? Okay. Per fortuna che c'è internet" — [00:19:34]

"C'è tanta gente che non sa niente, io veramente sono sconvolta. Una volta mi è arrivato uno e mi fa ma questo è il treno per per Roma? E dico no." — [00:19:48]

"...quindi c'è bisogno di qualcuno che parli e... Non c'è." — [00:20:10]

Her own reading: too few resources

Asked if she wanted to add anything, P1 defended the operators: in her view public transport is hard to run and the real problem is a lack of resources, which she sees on boats, trains and buses alike.

"Io sinceramente penso che sia molto difficile da gestire come cosa, quindi sono anche troppo bravi da un certo punto di vista. 2º me non hanno abbastanza risorse per per gestirli al meglio." — [00:20:33]
