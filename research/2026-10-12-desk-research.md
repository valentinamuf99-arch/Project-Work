# Desk research · benchmark of the apps participants mention

## Scope and method

This benchmark covers the five apps that came up in the interviews: Trenitalia (app and website), ViaggiaTreno, Trainline, AVM Venezia and SVT Vicenza. Google Maps, mentioned by P2 for bus alternatives, appears only in the comparison table. For each app it records the core commuting features, how it handles disruptions (real-time information, delay causes, notifications, alternatives) and its main UX strengths and weaknesses.

Evidence comes from official store listings and operator pages, trade press and a 2023 document of the transport regulator (ART), read on 2026-10-12. What participants said is reported from the interview notes, with the participant code. The plan asks for the benchmark to be verified on a phone: that has not been done yet, so every point marked "unverified" is still to be tested and the note stays `needs-review`.

## Trenitalia: official tickets, notifications that arrive late

All six participants mention Trenitalia, mostly to buy tickets and passes (P2, P3, P5). P6 buys the pass on the website and does not use the app.

- **Commuting features**: single tickets and regional passes, journey search, an "Infomobilità" section, and Smart Caring, a free service that follows up to four trains by number and sends push notifications.
- **Disruptions, as published**: Smart Caring sends the departure platform, delays and line problems with the reason. Since 15 May 2024, holders of a regional digital ticket also receive information on cancellations, delays and rerouting for the train they are on.
- **Disruptions, as experienced**: the email arrives late or about two minutes before the on-board announcement (P1, P3); a cancelled train is shown as cancelled only long after, so a delay cannot be told from a cancellation (P2); a line fault produced no notice at all (P4); strikes appear on the website but not in the app (P4); the network-wide banner is not filtered to the user's journey and is ignored (P2).
- **UX strengths**: the official source, linked to the ticket and the pass; following a train by number is supported.
- **UX weaknesses**: a gap between what the service promises and what participants live; ticket sales close a few minutes before departure (P2, P5); refund and rebooking rules require a Google search every time (P2); P6 met bugs once and stopped using the app.
- **Unverified**: whether Smart Caring is active on every Veneto regional line, and whether pass holders receive it without setting it up. No participant mentioned it.

## ViaggiaTreno: accurate real-time running, found by word of mouth

Only P3 uses ViaggiaTreno. She heard of it from a fellow student and trusts it more than the Trenitalia app for real-time status.

- **Commuting features**: search by train number, with actual arrival and departure times stop by stop, and station departure and arrival boards. No tickets.
- **Disruptions**: strong on real-time running ("almost always accurate", P3). No notifications, no alternatives, and the cause of a delay is rarely detailed.
- **UX strengths**: accurate, fast, no account needed.
- **UX weaknesses**: a dated website ("poorly made", P3); the user must know the train number and check it actively; almost nobody knows it exists.
- **Unverified**: this section relies on prior knowledge of the site, not on a check made for this note, including whether an official mobile app still exists.

## Trainline: the most polished interface, train only

Only P5 uses Trainline, for single same-day tickets, because she finds it more convenient.

- **Commuting features**: tickets from several operators (Trenitalia and Italo) and "Orari Live", real-time status of Trenitalia and Italo trains with platform, delays and cancellations, a live tracker of the train's position, and search by station or train number.
- **Disruptions**: delay and cancellation alerts before and during the journey, notifications for booked trips. A 2023 ART decision records Trenitalia's commitment to extend push information for regional passengers, including replacement buses and alternative routes, to tickets bought through platforms such as Trainline and Omio.
- **UX strengths**: the cleanest interface of the five, with real-time information and the ticket in the same place.
- **UX weaknesses**: no buses or boats.
- **Unverified**: whether Trainline sells Veneto regional passes (probably not, which would leave pass holders without ticket-linked information), and whether the regional push information committed in 2023 is live today.

## AVM Venezia: a ticket wallet for Venice

Only P1 uses AVM Venezia, for boat tickets linked to her resident card.

- **Commuting features**: purchase and validation of AVM/Actv tickets by QR code, a trip planner, timetables by stop, mobility news, payment for parking.
- **Disruptions**: general news only. The store listing mentions neither real-time arrivals nor push notifications.
- **UX strengths**: linked to the resident card; covers several Venice modes (bus, tram, boat).
- **UX weaknesses**: rated 1.2 out of 5 on the App Store (173 ratings); a user report says some vehicles have no QR reader and accept only RFID cards (the report is undated).
- **Unverified**: real-time information and notifications.

## SVT: tickets on the phone, no information found

Only P1 uses SVT Vicenza, for bus tickets.

- **Commuting features**: ticket purchase in the SVT Vicenza app. A second app, SVT ChiamaBus, books the evening on-demand service in the Vicenza urban area.
- **Disruptions**: no evidence found of real-time information, delay causes or notifications.
- **UX strengths**: the ticket is on the phone.
- **UX weaknesses**: a ticket tool, not an information tool, split across two apps. P2 says bus apps in general are much worse than train apps.
- **Unverified**: real-time information and notifications.

## Signals

This unit is reading, not evidence: it maps the benchmark to the brief's three moments and to the "when the routine breaks" concept.

**Gaps no app covers well**

- **Noticing**: no app tells an ordinary delay from a break early enough. A delay and a cancellation look the same until it is too late (P1, P2, P4).
- **Multi-operator journeys**: a boat, a train and a bus mean three apps and three tickets (P1). No app knows that a train delay breaks the bus connection.
- **Deciding**: no app proposes an alternative in the moment together with the rules, for example whether the pass is valid on it. Replacement buses did not accept the pass (P3); rebooking rules need a Google search (P2).
- **Strikes**: participants learn of them from Google, Instagram, Telegram or the conductor (P2, P4, P5), not linked to their own journey.
- **Understanding**: the cause and the expected duration come from people (staff, conductor), not from apps (P1, P4, P6).
- **People outside the apps**: P6 uses no app at all; P2, P3 and P5 open one only to buy tickets and otherwise rely on the board.

**Opportunities for the concept**

- **One personal alert with no setup**: learn the usual journey from the pass or from habits, stay silent on ordinary days, speak only when the routine is at risk ("only my journeys, not spam", P2) and before leaving home (P2, P5).
- **A break detector**: combine real-time running data, of the kind ViaggiaTreno shows, with the user's connections, to say "you will miss the Mestre connection" instead of "+12 min".
- **A "what are my options" card**: wait, change route or give up, each with whether the ticket or pass is valid, including replacement buses and nearby stations. The decision stays with the user.
- **Strikes on the user's own calendar**: which of their usual trains are guaranteed and which are not.
- **Notifying others**: a ready message for an employer, a lecturer or family (P4 called her parents four times in one month).
- **A channel outside the app** for people like P6: SMS or the station itself.
- **Dependency**: all of this needs open data (GTFS, GTFS Realtime) for Trenitalia, SVT and Busitalia Veneto, which has not been checked yet.
