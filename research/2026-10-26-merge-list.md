# Merged concept list · clustered by direction

## Scope and method

Two concept lists were merged into one, one row per concept, and the merged list was clustered by direction. Nothing new was invented: every row comes from one of the two lists.

- **Author marks.** `[me]` is a concept only in the human list, `[agent]` only in the agent list, `[both]` a concept that appears in both lists, even if it is worded differently or written for a different question.
- **Numbering.** Agent concepts keep their numbers 1 to 36 from `2026-10-26-possible-solutions-ai.md`. The one human concept with no agent match is number 37.
- **Questions.** #1 is the early commuter before six, #4 the evening student and the strike, #3 the plan in the unclear minutes. All four human concepts answer #1.
- **Direction.** Two concepts share a family when they answer the question in the same way. The agent file's directions A to H were not reused as given: two of them were joined (see "Differences from the agent's directions").

## Signals

This unit is a reading of the pattern, not of the sources.

- **Every human concept is about information.** All four land in the three families where the answer is to tell the commuter something: the fact, its likelihood, or the word of other riders. None changes who decides, what failure costs, or how the service runs.
- **The agent-only families all move the decision or its weight.** Planning ahead, the service deciding, lower stakes, a better service: these change the situation around the information, and only the agent list reaches them.
- **Every human concept answers #1 only.** The mixed families hold human concepts for #1 alone; #3 and #4 are agent-only, which limits the comparison.
- **The real-time view (37) is the only concept no agent concept matches.** The agent file had discarded a similar idea as belonging to another question; the human list kept it for #1.
- **Two `[both]` concepts were marked `[risky]` in the agent file** (2 and 27). The human list reached them independently, but that is not evidence that commuters want them.
