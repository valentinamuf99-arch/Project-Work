# Desk research · commuting in Veneto in numbers

## Scope and method

This note sizes the problem the brief is about. It answers three questions with public figures: **how many people commute** in Veneto, **how often regional trains are late or cancelled**, and **how much time is at stake**.

Sources were read on 2026-10-13. Istat and Trenitalia PDFs were downloaded and their text extracted; figures are copied from the tables, with the table name. Where a figure is an estimate made for this note, it is labelled **estimate** and the calculation is shown.

`grade` is `secondary` although most figures are official: the punctuality figures are **targets**, not measured results, and the 2026 ridership figures reach this note through the press.

## Limits of this evidence

- **Measured punctuality for Veneto is not public in readable form.** Both Trenitalia service charters have a column "Consuntivo" (actual result), but it is empty in the extracted text of both PDFs. Only the targets ("Obiettivo") could be read. The actual figures may be in an image or simply not filled in; this was not resolved.
- **No official count of cancelled trains for Veneto** was found. The charters do not report it, and the RFI booklet reports punctuality only.
- **No count of strike days** for 2025 or 2026 was found from an official source (the Commissione di Garanzia calendar was not checked).
- **Official punctuality excludes strikes and "eventi eccezionali"**, which are exactly the breaks the brief is about. The figures describe the ordinary day, not the bad day.
- **Mode and travel time by region are last published for 2011.** The 2019 and 2021 Istat releases give counts but not the train share for Veneto.
- **No source gives average minutes lost per delayed trip.** Time lost is estimated below from thresholds, not measured.

## How many commute: 2.7 million daily trips for work or study

Istat, permanent census 2019 (release of 13 May 2021, Prospetto 1): **2,690,173** Veneto residents travel every day to their place of study or work, **55.1%** of the resident population, the fourth highest share in Italy (national 50.7%). Of these, **1,904,560** travel for work (70.8%) and **785,613** for study (29.2%).

**1,455,575** of them (**54.1%**) leave their own municipality, one of the highest shares in Italy (national 42.5%).

For comparison, the 2011 census counted 2,603,830 daily commuters in Veneto (1,810,278 for work, 793,552 for study).

The 2021 origin-destination matrices for work (published 2 October 2025) and study (22 September 2026) exist in the Istat data browser, but their regional totals were not extracted for this note.

## How they travel: the train is a small minority

Istat, census 2011, Prospetto 11 (main mode for the longest leg, Veneto, % of daily commuters):

| Mode | Veneto | Italy |
|---|---|---|
| Train, tram, metro | **2.6%** | 4.7% |
| Urban or extra-urban bus | 8.3% | 8.7% |
| Company or school bus | 3.3% | 2.9% |
| Car, as driver | **50.0%** | 44.9% |
| Car, as passenger | 14.1% | 15.9% |
| Bicycle | 7.0% | 3.3% |
| On foot | 10.9% | 15.8% |

**Estimate**: 2.6% of 2,603,830 is about **68,000 daily train commuters in Veneto in 2011**. The share is only for those who reported the train as their main mode, so mixed trips (car to the station, as for P5 and P6) may be partly counted as car.

The Veneto Region's 2023 figures (press release of 26 September 2024) point the same way: motorised transport is **80.2%** of all trips, mostly by car, and public transport **6.8%**. These are all trips, not only commuting.

## Who uses the regional train today: about 166,000 passengers a day

Trenitalia, Carta dei Servizi Veneto, section on the service ("Offerta"):

| | Charter 2025 | Charter 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Trains per day | 667 | 643 |
| Passengers per day | 172,215 | 165,963 |
| Passengers per year | 46,684,150 | 46,805,604 |
| Buses per day | 115 | 118 |

The charters do not say which year these figures refer to. The 2026 drop in trains and daily passengers may reflect lines moved to a separate charter (Adria–Venezia, Rovigo–Chioggia, Rovigo–Verona have their own); this was not checked.

According to the Corriere del Veneto (21 September 2026), as reported by AltoVicentinOnline, Trenitalia's 2026 figures show regional passengers **up 1.5%** and **passes up 1.8%** year to date, with about 700,000 more trips projected on a total of 46 million.

## How long the commute takes: 54 minutes a day, longer for students

- **Today, all trips**: in 2023 Veneto residents aged 14 to 84 spent on average **54 minutes a day** travelling, up from 51 in 2022 (Veneto Region, "Stili di mobilità in Veneto").
- **2011, commuters only** (Istat, Prospetti 7 to 9, one-way trip, Veneto):

| One-way time | All | Work | Study |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 15 min | 56.5% | 53.6% | 63.2% |
| 16 to 30 min | 27.0% | 30.6% | 18.7% |
| 31 to 45 min | 7.6% | 7.8% | 7.1% |
| 46 to 60 min | 4.8% | 4.5% | 5.6% |
| Over 60 min | 4.1% | 3.5% | **5.4%** |

Veneto has the second highest share in Italy of students travelling over an hour (5.4%, after Lazio at 7.1%). Train commuters, who cover longer distances, are likely to sit in the longer bands, but the 2011 tables do not cross mode with time.

| Indicator | Target 2025 | Target 2026 |
|---|---|---|

| Winter, arrival within 15 min | 98.0% | 98.0% |

## How often trains are late: what is reported as achieved

- **Trenitalia, all causes**: at a presentation in Marghera on 28 February 2025, Trenitalia's regional business director reported **94%** punctuality in Veneto including all causes, and **99%** counting only causes attributable to Trenitalia, "in continuità con il 2024" (ANSA). The period and threshold were not stated.
- **RFI, Italy**: real punctuality of regional trains in 2024 (arrival within 5 minutes, all causes) was **88.9%**, down 0.9 points from 2023; monthly values fell to about 88% from May. Excluding external causes (weather, people on the line, theft), it was 90.9%. Italy runs on average 7,603 regional trains a day. Veneto is one of five regions producing 17 to 22 million train-km a year. The booklet's regional chart could not be read as text, so no Veneto figure from RFI is reported here.
- **Independent monitoring, Belluno lines** (trenibelluno.it, 2024, arrival within 5 minutes, cancellations counted as late, strikes excluded): Belluno–Conegliano–Venezia **93.0%**, Belluno–Calalzo 95.9%, Belluno–Montebelluna–Treviso 96.4%, Montebelluna–Padova 94.8%. On the Venezia line, delays of 6 to 15 minutes and cancellations increased compared with the two previous years.

## What the rules count as a break

The 2026 charter sets two thresholds that define, in practice, when the operator owes the commuter something:

- **Pass holders** (section 8.4): compensation of 10% of the monthly pass for each month in which **10% or more of the trains** on the route **arrive more than 15 minutes late or are cancelled**. Amounts under €4 are not paid.
- **Long delays** (section 8.2): for a delay over **60 minutes**, a missed connection or a cancellation, Trenitalia must communicate an alternative within **100 minutes** of the scheduled departure; otherwise the passenger can claim the documented cost of reaching the destination by other public transport. Single tickets get 25% back for delays of 60 to 119 minutes and 50% from 120 minutes (tickets of €16 or more only).

## Estimate: what the targets mean for one commuter

These are calculations made for this note, assuming delays are spread evenly over trains. They describe the **ordinary day only**, because strikes and exceptional events are excluded from the targets.

A commuter making 2 train trips a day, 20 days a month, makes **40 trips a month**.

| If the target is met | Trips late per month |
|---|---|
| 94% within 5 min (region, winter) | about **2.4** trips more than 5 min late |
| 92% within 5 min (Venezia–Verona, Venezia–Bologna) | about **3.2** trips more than 5 min late |
| 97% within 15 min (peak hours) | about **1.2** trips more than 15 min late |
| 88.9% within 5 min (RFI Italy 2024, actual) | about **4.4** trips more than 5 min late |

The pass compensation threshold (10% of trains over 15 minutes late or cancelled) equals **4 trips a month**. At the official targets, a pass holder would see about 1.2: the compensation rule is set at **more than three times** the expected rate, so an ordinary bad month pays nothing.

At regional scale, with 643 trains and about 166,000 passengers a day: if 6% of trains arrive more than 5 minutes late, that is about **39 trains** and, at average loads, about **10,000 passenger trips a day**; if 2% arrive more than 15 minutes late, about **13 trains** and **3,300 passenger trips a day**. Peak trains are fuller than average, so the real passenger count is likely higher.

## Estimate: time at stake

No source gives average minutes of delay. A floor can be set from the thresholds: a trip more than 15 minutes late costs at least 15 minutes. With about 1.2 such trips a month at peak, a commuter loses **at least 18 minutes a month** to long delays on the ordinary day, on top of shorter delays. This is small against the 54-minute daily average, which supports what participants say: the **ordinary delay is absorbed**, and the cost sits in the rare break (a cancellation, a missed connection, a strike) that the official figures exclude. P2 measures a cancellation as up to an hour ("La cancellazione può significare un ritardo di un'ora se devi aspettare quello dopo"); P4's line fault lasted an hour and a half before the cause was known.

## Signals

This unit is a reading of the figures above, not what the sources say.

- **The population is large, the train share small.** About 2.7 million Veneto residents commute daily and over half leave their municipality, but in 2011 only about 2.6% used the train as their main mode. Train commuters are a minority inside a car region, which is why family cars are the fallback in the interviews (P2, P4, P6).
- **Participants travel on the region's least punctual lines.** Venezia–Verona (Padova, Vicenza) and Venezia–Bologna (Padova, Monselice, Rovigo) have the lowest punctuality targets in Veneto, and the peak hours have lower targets than the rest of the day.
- **Ordinary delays are frequent and expected.** At target, a daily commuter meets 2 to 3 late trips a month, more on the trunk lines. This matches P5's "five to seven delays in about three weeks" and the general habituation in the interviews.
- **The breaks are invisible in the official numbers.** Strikes and exceptional events are excluded from punctuality; cancellations are not reported. The events the brief is about are, by construction, outside the measurement. Their frequency has to come from the interviews (for example P4: four times in one month last semester) or from independent monitoring.
- **The compensation threshold is out of reach.** A pass holder is compensated only in a month three times worse than the target, which may explain why no participant mentions it.
