# Desk research · App Store reviews of Trenitalia and Trainline

## Scope and method

Trenitalia is the app participants mention most (all six, P1 to P6). Trainline is mentioned only by P5, but it is the only other train app in the interviews, so it is included as a comparison. On 2026-10-13 the "most recent" reviews of both apps were read on the Italian App Store and captured as screenshots: 21 for Trenitalia (`research/raw/Trenitalia screenshot reviews/`, IMG_2375 to IMG_2395) and 12 for Trainline (`research/raw/Trainline screenshot reviews/`, IMG_2396 to IMG_2407). That is roughly 45 Trenitalia and 30 Trainline reviews, mostly dated May to October 2026, many with a reply from the developer.

The reviews were read in full and grouped by theme. Quotes are verbatim in Italian, followed by an English gloss, and cite the screenshot file instead of a timestamp. Reviewer usernames are not reported: reviewers are not participants, and some handles look like real names.

## Limits of this evidence

- **Self-selection**: people mostly write a review when something has gone wrong, so the sample leans negative. The few five-star reviews are short.
- **Unknown reviewers**: there is no way to know who is a commuter, where they travel or whether the story is accurate. Some say they are commuters ("pendolare"); only one review names a Veneto line (Venezia–Trieste).
- **Partial screenshots**: some reviews are cut at the top or bottom of a screenshot and are used only where the readable part is clear.
- **Use**: this note can support or question patterns from the interviews and point to new ones to check. It does not carry the same weight as the interviews, which is why `grade` is `secondary` and `confidence` is `medium`.

## Real-time information is wrong when it matters

Several reviews say the live status is unreliable. One commuter was sent to platform 11 at Bologna while the train left from the opposite side of the station, and the conductor advised not to rely on the app.

> "Dopo aver chiesto al capotreno spiegazioni mi ha consigliato di non affidarmi all'applicazione per la consultazione." [IMG_2389] (the conductor told me not to rely on the app)

> "Purtroppo l'aggiornamento in tempo reale è sempre sbagliato. Linea Venezia Trieste il personale dì bordo non aggiorna correttamente" [IMG_2383] (real-time updates are always wrong on the Venezia–Trieste line)

On Trainline, delays are reported as rarely shown, or shown when there is none [IMG_2401], and the train's position as wrong ("siamo a termini e dice che siamo a san gennaro", we are at Termini and it says San Gennaro) [IMG_2401]. One user's app stopped updating on the very train they were on, after a 33-minute delay that made them miss the change [IMG_2403].

## Notifications are noise, and season-ticket holders get none for their own route

One Trenitalia user receives 8 to 10 identical morning notifications about night works, partly in English, and switched them off, losing the useful alerts about faults on the line too [IMG_2378].

Another, a pass holder, says single-ticket buyers get trip notifications but pass holders do not, even for cancellations and route changes on the route the pass covers. The consequence is concrete:

> "Almeno una volta al mese mi ritrovo a piedi o se va bene arrivare a lavoro in ritardo - dovendo comunque prendere l'auto." [IMG_2386] (at least once a month I'm left on foot, or at best late for work, having to take the car anyway)

## The commuter's main task is checking, not buying

Trainline replaced the Orario Treni app, and a large share of its recent reviews come from former Orario Treni users. They list what they lost: search by train number, a station board of arrivals and departures with platforms and delays, saved stations, saved recurring routes, and a single screen with the train's progress [IMG_2397, IMG_2399, IMG_2400, IMG_2406, IMG_2407].

> "In generale l'esperienza d'uso è troppo incentrata sull'acquisto di un biglietto e poco sul verificare i treni disponibili o i loro eventuali ritardi" [IMG_2407] (the experience is too centred on buying a ticket and too little on checking trains and delays)

> "Sono un pendolare, e ho bisogno di monitorare la situazione dei treni nella mia stazione per capire l'andamento del mio treno e quale mi convenga prendere." [IMG_2400] (I'm a commuter and need to monitor my station to see how my train is doing and which one to take)

The same users stress speed in a hurry: "quando si è di corsa (letteralmente) risulta difficile schiacciare ottocento cose" (when you are literally running it is hard to tap eight hundred things) [IMG_2396]. The same pattern appears for Trenitalia: one regular user finds the live train view "meno intuitiva e più dispersiva" (less intuitive, more scattered) after an update [IMG_2384].

## Ticket rules ignore delays

Trenitalia closes ticket sales 5 minutes before the scheduled departure, even when the train is late. One user at the station at 19:00, with the 19:05 train 10 minutes late, could not buy a ticket for it and had to buy one for the next train, at the risk of a fine for boarding the earlier one [IMG_2376]. A second user reports the same rule and a fine received once [IMG_2392]. Trenitalia replies that the limit comes from regulation, not from the app.

## After a cancellation, recovery is pushed onto the user

- **Refund and compensation**: the "indennizzo" button returns "Informazione al momento non disponibile" for days, and the website form freezes on "motivo della richiesta" [IMG_2387]. A user who bought a ticket a few minutes before a cancellation found they had no right to a refund [IMG_2388]. Trenitalia's replies send users to the call center, a web form or the Smart Refund platform [IMG_2378, IMG_2387, IMG_2388].
- **Changing train**: "gestisci biglietto" fails with "qualcosa è andato storto"; the user calls it "Funzione indispensabile per un pendolare" (essential for a commuter) [IMG_2377].
- **Missing ticket**: a paid ticket never appeared; after five staff members and a 40-minute call center wait, the user paid twice [IMG_2393].
- **Trainline**: the platform says compensation is handled by the rail company, the insurance sold with the ticket did not pay for missed connections, and a refund for a bus that never ran was refused because the operator refused it [IMG_2398, IMG_2402].

One review title sums it up: "Muro di gomma" (a rubber wall) [IMG_2375].

## Connections and alternatives

- Both apps propose tight connections. Trenitalia users ask why the app sells connections the trains cannot keep [IMG_2385] and suggest offering alternatives with wider connection times [IMG_2381]. A Trainline user is offered 5-minute connections on long routes, "che anche se i treni fossero in orario si farebbe fatica" (hard even if trains were on time) [IMG_2397].
- After a missed connection, no alternative is offered: "non mi ha inviato neanche delle alternative di treni che avrei potuto prendere per tornare" [IMG_2403].
- Replacement buses: one did not stop at the station where the ticket started [IMG_2379]; a Trainline user did not know where to catch one or which operator ran it [IMG_2404]. A route suspended for planned works in July and August was not flagged before purchase [IMG_2394].

## For the user, the app is the service

Many one-star reviews are about delays, dirty trains and prices, not app features. Trenitalia's standard reply asks users to rate "solamente i servizi dell'app" (only the app's features) [IMG_2377, IMG_2379, IMG_2380, IMG_2385, IMG_2391]. Users do not make that distinction: the app is where they meet the service, so its failures are rated there.

> "su 6 treni della giornata, 5 erano in ritardo di almeno mezz'ora, non usufruirò più del servizio Trenitalia" [IMG_2377] (5 of 6 trains today were at least half an hour late; I won't use Trenitalia again)

## Updates break habits

"Peggiorata con aggiornamento" (worse after the update) is a recurring title for both apps. Regular users report buttons that do not respond, purchase flows that ask again for saved data, and a navigation they find more complicated [IMG_2383, IMG_2384, IMG_2390, IMG_2391]. A commuter works by habit, so every change to the layout has a cost.

## What users value

The few positive reviews value having tickets always at hand, real-time updates when they work, a fast purchase [IMG_2388], and, for Trainline, an overview of all the travel options [IMG_2398].

## Signals

This unit is reading, not evidence: it maps the reviews to the brief's three moments and to the interview notes.

**Patterns confirmed by the interviews**

- **Noticing**: no warning, or a late one, before a break. Reviews: pass holders get no alerts [IMG_2386]. Interviews: an email two minutes before the announcement (P1), no notice of a line fault (P4), strikes never notified (P2), warnings only a few minutes ahead (P3).
- **Ticket sales closing before departure**: also in the P2 and P5 notes. The reviews add the case where the train is late and the rule still applies.
- **Missed connections and no alternative proposed**: the main break point for P1 and P2 (the change at Mestre). The reviews show the apps themselves sell tight connections.
- **Replacement buses that do not work as alternatives**: also in the P3, P4 and P6 notes.
- **Trust in people over the app**: a conductor tells a reviewer not to trust the app [IMG_2389]; in the interviews the conductor is often the one who explains (P3, P4).

**New or stronger in the reviews**

- **Pass holders as second-class users** of notifications: worth asking P3, P5 and P6, who hold passes.
- **Checking vs buying**: the strongest theme for Trainline. It matches the brief's moment before departure: a station board, a train by number, saved routes, one screen, fast.
- **Recovery after the break** (refund, change of ticket, compensation) is a fourth moment the brief does not name, and it is where reviews are angriest.
- **Notification fatigue**: generic alerts get switched off, and useful ones are lost with them. This supports P2's "only my journeys".

**Opportunities for the concept**

- Alerts tied to the pass and the usual route, silent on ordinary days.
- A commuter view built for checking, not for buying: my station, my train, my connection, in one screen.
- When a connection is at risk, offer the next feasible option with wider margins, and say whether the ticket is valid on it.
- After a cancellation, carry the user into the refund or change from the same place, without sending them to another channel.
