## Who she is and what her commute looks like

Participant P1 commutes to work from the Lido di Venezia to Torri di Quartesolo, near Vicenza. She uses three modes in sequence: the boat from the Lido, the train to Vicenza and the bus from Vicenza to Torri di Quartesolo. Without delays the journey takes about two hours and forty minutes; with problems it can take longer. She says a closer railway stop exists, but there is no bus from there to Torri, so she always gets off at Vicenza.

> "Sì, allora ci metto circa due ore e mezzo in totale, quasi tre e dipende." — [--:--:--]

In her view the weak point of the journey is not any single mode but the change from one to the next.

> "però sinceramente il problema sono le coincidenze" — [--:--:--]

## Three apps, used almost only for tickets

The participant buys every ticket on her phone, with a different app for each mode: AVM Venezia for the boat (with her resident card), the Trenitalia app for the train and the SVT Vicenza app for the bus. On ordinary days she opens each app at the start of the leg to validate the ticket, and the Trenitalia app only to show the ticket to the inspector. On the day of the cancellation described below, she opened the app to look for alternative trains.

> "ecco lì un altro motivo in cui ho preso l'oppinazione per vedere se c'erano dei treni che volevo prendere per andare." — [--:--:--]

At the station she prefers to look at the departure boards rather than her phone, which she keeps in her pocket.

> "allora guardo i monitor e vedo 20 minuti, 30 minuti, cancellato, cosa sta succedendo" — [--:--:--]

## The bus's ordinary delay

The bus delay is not a problem for her: she takes it for granted and plans her connections around it, choosing the train or the bus with that margin in mind.

> "l'autobus insia è sempre un po' ritardo, quindi non è un problema." — [--:--:--]

## How she notices something is wrong

The participant has no single signal. On the boat and at the stops, the first clue is the crowd: many people mean a run was skipped or a delay has built up. On the train she notices when the train stands still and does not leave.

> "capisco che magari il treno non c'è qualcosa che non va quando sta fermo. Semplicemente salgo e non parte." — [--:--:--]

## The last disrupted journey: the board did not show it

The most recent episode is a journey home. At the station the board showed many trains delayed or cancelled, but not hers, so she waited. Her train instead arrived at least fifteen minutes late, a delay that was not on the board when she checked it. The train arrived full of people.

## The cancellation between Padova and Mestre, announced by email

During the run, between Padova and Mestre, the train stopped several times without her understanding why. The only information came by email, because she had bought the ticket in the app, about two minutes before the announcement on board: the train was cancelled and would terminate at Mestre.

## The alternative found by chance

At Mestre all passengers got off. The participant had resigned herself to taking a bus, but in the underpass she saw on the boards a train to Venezia that had not yet left, and other people running towards the platform. She ran too and got on; other passengers followed her. Nobody had told her about this option. As she boarded, she was afraid that train would be cancelled as well.

> "Assolutamente no, no, no. È stato rando" — [--:--:--]

> "ho visto l'ora, non è partito, ho visto gente che correva per andare" — [--:--:--]

## The knock-on effect on the boat

The accumulated delay made her miss the boat connection: once in Venezia she waited half an hour for the boat to the Lido.

> "quando sono arrivata non c'era il battello. Mi ha dovuto aspettare mezz'ora per il battello" — [--:--:--]

## The hardest part: being stuck at Mestre

Asked about the hardest part, the participant speaks of some panic. She was not afraid of not getting home at all, but of being stuck in the evening in a station she does not know well and where she does not feel safe, alone, with a bus at an inconvenient time. Nobody could come and pick her up.

> "È più il fatto che mi sarei fermata in una stazione che a me non piace per niente." — [--:--:--]

## Living without knowing when she will arrive

Living on the Lido and depending only on public transport, a cancellation upsets the rest of her journey, boats included. She describes this not knowing as a constant condition, not only on the day of the cancellation.

> "se mi cancellano un treno, una parte, mi sballa completamente tutto." — [--:--:--]

> "Io ogni volta che prendo l'autobus, o comunque quando vengo, io non so quando torno. Non so quando arrivo." — [--:--:--]

## How often it happens

Delays are constant, but a break like the one at Mestre is rare: two or three times a year. That day was the worst in recent times.

> "Non è che capita spesso, non capita spesso perché allora ritardo dicene sono sempre, ma non così. Così capiterà. 2-3 volte l'anno." — [--:--:--]

> "Sì, di questi ultimo tempi è stato il proprio peggiore assoluto." — [--:--:--]

## What she would have wanted to know

The participant would have wanted to know three things: why the train was stopped, how long it would last and which trains she could take to reach Venezia. For her the reason matters because it helps estimate the timing, since different causes mean different waits. She adds that she knows Mestre, but a traveller who does not know it would not know where to go.

> "Cioè perché mi blocchi il treno?" — [--:--:--]

> "Fammi capire qual è il problema, qual è la tempistica" — [--:--:--]

## Nobody warns, nobody to ask

Asked whether someone or something has ever warned her of a problem or suggested what to do during the journey, she says no. She also says there are no staff on the train to turn to: over the whole time she has done her current commute she has seen the inspector three times. She uses the internet to find information, and other travellers often ask her for information.

> "No, cioè ora non è mai successo assolutamente." — [--:--:--]

> "ho visto il controllore del treno tre volte." — [--:--:--]

## The underlying problem, in her view

At the end the participant partly defends the operators: in her view public transport is very hard to manage, and the problem is a lack of resources, which she sees on boats, trains and buses. In the context of the interview she links it to the absence of staff to turn to.
